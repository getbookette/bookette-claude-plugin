# 3. Propose line items

Write `out/ledger.json`, a list of objects:

```json
{
  "source_file": "inbox/plumber_invoice.pdf",
  "date": "2026-02-17",
  "vendor": "Mile High Plumbing",
  "description": "Kitchen drain repair",
  "kind": "expense | income",
  "amount": "412.00",
  "currency": "USD",
  "entity": "entity key from profile.json, or null",
  "schedule": "E | C",
  "line": "14",
  "trip": "trip key or null",
  "meal": false,
  "tag": "repair | capital | null",
  "confidence": "high | medium | low",
  "status": "confirmed | flagged | unclassified"
}
```

Do not write an `id`: the assignment step below gives every addition a code-owned id from the item's stable facts, so two runs that propose the same facts for the same items number them the same way. The assignment step adds them.

- `confirmed` — the line is placed, with or without an entity. It joins the next run's confirmed digest, so a later answer can assign the entity or the category. The importer may lower it to `flagged` when the source could not be read; that hold is the importer's, and the line still counts in the totals.
- `flagged` — a line you flagged counts in the totals when it has an entity, the report shows "Needs a look", and the next run stages it as held. A held line takes no `ledger_changes`.
- `unclassified` — the same placement and the same held rule, and the report shows "Not classified".
- A line with no entity is out of the entity totals whichever word it carries. The word is `confirmed` when an answer should assign it.
- A line with no entity is mapped by description, like any line: it carries the schedule its description fits, `C` or `E`, and a line of that schedule's table in `schedule_lines.json` — never a `null` schedule or line. That schedule is provisional: the next run's digest carries `schedule: null` for the line until an answer assigns its entity, and the move then chooses the line again (step 5). A document that names no place is never placed by timing alone — its date near a trip's or another document's books nothing (`reference/situations.md`): step 4 asks which place it belongs to — a found-place question, `unassigned_line_entity_q` or an `open_` key — and when no answer could settle it, step 7 writes the `entity_unestablished` finding.
- `source_file` names the original the line comes from. Where the batch's originals sit in `inbox/`, it and every other row that names an original (a donation, a home cost, a tax payment's `payment_source` and `bill_source`, a `source_file` change's `to`) name it by `inbox/<path>`, its folders and extension included, never by its reading's stem, its bare file name, its reading's path or another folder, which `check_outputs.js` refuses; a spelling the confirmed digest hands you, or a carried home cost's or payment's, is copied as booked.
- `date` is the day the document says the money moved — the day the amount was charged, paid or received: a receipt carries the date it prints. Where a document prints both a service period and a payment date, the payment date dates the line and the service period goes into the line's `description`; never re-date a line by its service period. Never leave a line out of `out/ledger.json` because its date is outside the tax year, except the installment `reference/situations.md` names, which is staged nowhere: the build lists the line in `books.json`'s `outside_year` and counts it in no total, and it needs no finding (step 7). A hotel folio prints the stay — the arrival and departure dates — and a posting date for each night; book its charges on the date the folio settles: the date of its payment line, or the departure date when it prints none — never the arrival date or one night's posting date. The stay's own dates go on the trip's segment (step 5). A form for the tax year that prints no date of its own — a 1099-NEC, a 1099-MISC or a 1098 — books on the tax year's last day (`2026-12-31` in a 2026 workspace), its corrected form too; `check_outputs.js` refuses another date. Step 1's duplicate candidates match a card row within three days of the line's date, and the build converts a foreign amount at the rate of the line's own date, so the settlement date is the one both need.
- `amount` is a decimal string in the document currency. Never convert currency yourself; `build_books.js` converts using `dist/scripts/fx.js`. Book a printed figure only, never one you computed: a hotel folio's lodging is one printed figure of room and tax alone: its total when it charges nothing else, else its printed room-and-tax subtotal, else each printed room and tax charge as a line of its own; its dining charges are `meal` lines of their own — never the total less the dining (`reference/situations.md`). `check_outputs.js` refuses a line whose amount its staged reading prints nowhere. A printed zero, such as a 1099 box of `0.00`, books no line and is asked nothing: it stays in the document's `fields.form_boxes` as printed, and `check_outputs.js` refuses a zero line.
- `currency` is the three-letter code the document prints. A reading that prints none (`currency: null`) is `USD` when the document's vendor, addresses and figures are US ones: write `USD` and say so in the document's `notes`. Doubt it when the document names a place outside the US, prints another currency's word or sign, or is dated inside a trip abroad: then book nothing from it, set it `needs_review` with its total in `fields`, and write a `note` finding naming the document and saying its currency is not printed. `check_outputs.js` refuses a line whose `currency` is not a code in capitals.
- Map each line to the line of its schedule that fits what its document says was bought or received, by the line labels in `rules/ty<tax_year>/schedule_lines.json`; the vendor's name decides nothing on its own, since one vendor sells what different lines carry. An item moved onto another schedule by an answer (step 5) is mapped the same way for the target schedule. The person's own pick of a line's category, a `correct_category:` answer, is the one binding category: Bookette applies it (step 5).
- Card and bank exports: one line item per business transaction; personal transactions and transfers (a payment to the card, a move between the person's own accounts) are not booked. Write `out/export_rows.json`, a list with one entry for each card or bank export this batch books or keeps as `support` row by row (every one but a code-read platform statement, below): `{"file": "<the export>", "rows": [{"row": 1, "disposition": "booked", "document": null}, …]}`, one disposition per transaction row, the rows counted from 1 in the order the export prints them — its staged reading's `transactions` carrying a date and an amount when it has one. An export with no staged reading is read by code from its file under the column roles you give in the entry's `table`: `{"header": [<the header row's cells as printed>] or null when it prints none, "columns": {"date", "posted", "description", "amount", "debit", "credit", "balance", "currency", "account"}, "date_order": "MDY"|"DMY"|"YMD", "decimal_mark": "."|",", "money_out": "negative"|"positive"|"debit_column"}`. Each column is the role's 0-based field position in a transaction record, or null: `date` is the date every row is dated by (the transaction date where the export prints one, else its only date column, whatever its header calls it), `posted` a second, posting date beside it; either `amount` (one signed column) or `debit` and `credit` (money out and money in in columns of their own), never both; `balance` a running balance. `money_out` says how money out prints: `negative`, `positive` (many card exports print a charge positive and a payment negative), or `debit_column`. Its rows are then the records below the header whose `date` field holds a date (a preamble, a blank line, a repeated header or a total is no row). Each row takes exactly one word:
  - `booked`: a business transaction no other document carries; its line (or the tax payment, home cost or donation staged from the export) is at the row's own amount and date.
  - `restated_by`: another document is this same payment's evidence — a receipt, invoice, folio, payment confirmation or donation receipt, of this batch or confirmed by an earlier run — whose reading prints exactly the row's amount, dated within seven days of it (a card posts days after the purchase, a folio's charge after checkout). That document books the line and the row books nothing; name it in `document`. The row's descriptor and the document must name the same merchant: a descriptor may spell it otherwise (`AMZN Mktp US*2K3` is Amazon.com; `SQ *` and `TST*` are point-of-sale prefixes), but equal cents on the same day from two different merchants are two payments, and a figure that only happens to equal the row's amount restates nothing. Each figure restates one row: two rows of one amount need two documents. A refund is never restated by the purchase's receipt, and a form never restates a row.
  - `support`: a deposit or payment a summing document of this batch covers without printing its amount — a 1099, a property manager's statement, a platform payout summary, a folio paid in parts; name it in `document`, and give that document's `out/documents.json` entry the total it prints, in `fields.total`, as copied from the page (of several, the one the rows are part of, such as a statement's owner-distribution total).
  - `personal`, `transfer` (money between the person's own accounts, or a payment to the card) or `foreign` (an amount in a currency other than US dollars): booked nowhere, `document` null.

  `check_outputs.js` refuses a `table` its figures contradict, by typed code (`export_table_invalid`: a header not printed, a dated row that does not read under the date order or roles, a balance the amounts do not run, an amount column that is itself a running balance), an export with a row missing or declared twice (`export_row_unaccounted`), a `booked` row no line from the export carries at its amount within seven days, one line per row (`export_row_booked_unmatched`), any other row whose amount the export still books within seven days (`export_row_booked_beside`), a `restated_by` row whose document carries no figure at its amount within seven days, one row per figure (`export_row_restated_unmatched`), and `support` rows whose document prints no total or whose rows sum past it (`export_row_support_exceeds`), naming the rows' positions and never their amounts. When an earlier run already booked the row from the export, the receipt arriving now is `near_duplicate_of: <the export>`; when every row of an export restates a confirmed line, the export is `near_duplicate_of` that line's document.

- A code-read platform payout statement (its staged page carries `fields.platform_statement`, reference/staged-readings.md) is the one card or bank export not booked row by row: run `platform_statement` for its figures and book each printed figure as one line, amounts exactly as printed, with the document's own date for the figure where it prints one (a payout or disbursement date on the grid) and the platform as the vendor. Gross, pass-through, incentive and refund figures are `kind: income`; the fee, seller-remitted-tax and withholding figures are `kind: expense`, one line per figure; the deposit, traveler-side-fee, platform-remitted-tax and net figures book nothing here — they stay in the document's support for the accountant. The check re-derives the same figures from the stored grid and refuses an amount it does not print, so never adjust, split or round one. When the script answers the mismatch rejection instead, book the page as any card export and add a `note` finding (staged-readings.md).
- A payout export you read yourself (no `fields.platform_statement`) books its gross once as income and each fee the platform kept as its own expense line. A pass-through column, such as a cleaning fee the guest paid, books as income of its own only when the page prints that it sits outside the income column you book: a printed breakdown that shows the income term and the fee as separate addends of a total, or a header that states that column excludes that fee or is the nightly amount alone. Then the figure you book as gross is the income term, never a sum that contains the fee. A header that states the income column includes that fee settles it the other way: book that column once as the gross and the pass-through column nothing. Anything else — a single grand total, a per-row `Total` whose composition is not printed, a column merely titled `Payout` — leaves it open; never add, subtract, multiply or divide the printed figures to settle it. While it is open, the person says which, but only while the export's gross books to exactly one place `profile.json` stages. Then book the gross there and each figure of every uncertain column as an income line of its own with `entity` `null`, under the same vendor as the gross, which keeps it out of every total. Ask the one `pass_through` question of step 4 citing exactly those lines: one answer settles every open column of the export. Its `own_income` answer books them to the gross's place and its `inside` answer settles them as already inside the gross (step 5). Otherwise there is no one place to offer: the gross books to no place, to several, or part of it waits with no entity for a question about its place. Then book the gross only, never an uncertain column, and write a `note` finding naming the document and its columns that cites the gross lines' ledger ids beside the document. This is the one note about a fact the person could answer (step 7): a later run cannot ask it, because the export's reading is not staged again. Write that note as the next bullet says, naming the columns too.
- Any document, read by hand or by code, whose placed income books to one place while other income under the same vendor waits with no entity for a question about its place takes one `note` finding, whether or not a pass-through column is open: a payout export covering a declared listing and one `profile.json` does not stage, or a bank export whose transfer rail carries a placed rent deposit and an unrelated deposit. The note names the document and the waiting income, and the open columns when one is, and cites the placed lines' ledger ids beside the document. It lets a found-place or `placement` question ask about the waiting income: `check_outputs.js` exempts the document it cites from the pass_through question's hold.
- **The places' standard addresses.** Write `out/places.json` first: one key per entity `profile.json` stages, the USPS Publication 28 standard form of its `address` — `{"usps_standard": "2207 ELM ST", "city": "Boulder", "region": "CO", "country": "US"}`, the delivery line alone in upper case with the Postal Service's suffix, directional and unit abbreviations and no punctuation, the postal region and the ISO country, each null when the address gives none — or `null` for a place with no `address`. Match a printed address to a place by comparing the reading's `usps_standard`, `city` and `region` with this file's, never by spelling rules of your own. `check_outputs.js` refuses a placement question while the file is missing or lacks a place with an address (`places_unwritten`), and an entry for an unstaged key, of another shape, or whose `usps_standard` drops the address's house number (`places_malformed`). It ties a tax bill to its property by this file too, so it refuses a purchase option writing a property's basis with no land value while the batch carries a tax bill and the property, staged with an address, has no well-formed entry here (`purchase_land_value`).
- A reading file's `hint_entity` is the place the person uploaded it for. Book its lines there unless the document names another of the person's places, by name, alias, address, listing or payer; a printed address names a place when the reading's `usps_standard`, `city` and `region` match that place's in `out/places.json`. Then leave `entity` null so step 4 asks, offering both. `check_outputs.js` refuses a line from a hinted reading booked to any other place unless its `answer_keys` names an answer in which the person chose that place.
- A reading file's `attach_to` naming a `trip` is a receipt the person attached to that trip: book its lines as usual, with `trip` null on every one — never a trip of its own; the importer ties them (`check_outputs.js` refuses a model-written `trip` on such a line, `attach_target_mismatch`).
- A reading file's `attach_to` naming a `line` ledger id is a receipt the person attached to that line: book none of its lines, and record the document `support` — the line it names is the money event and this receipt is its document (`check_outputs.js` refuses a line from such a reading, `attach_line_books_nothing`).
- Meals: set `"meal": true`. Travel within a trip window: set `trip` to the trip's `key` in the profile, never its `name` (`check_outputs.js` refuses a name). An item tagged with a trip takes that trip's entity — the trip's entity is established by its answer, the profile or the place step 5 records it under, and the item does not book to a different business.
- Trips are read from the documents and recorded (step 5), never asked, when a document points to their place; a trip no document places is asked which place it was for (step 4's Trips no document places paragraph). A trip is days away from the travel home (`profile.json`'s `home`, or the one step 5 finds) in another city. Its segments come from the documents: an airline e-ticket's legs give the cities and the days out and back (several legs are one trip, a segment per stop, each starting on the day its leg lands and ending on the day the next leg leaves); a hotel folio's arrival and departure give the stay; with neither, the days of the receipts in that city, or of a mileage log naming the drive, give it. Fares and rides that print no city (card rows) date no trip on their own, since no document says where it went: book them on their travel lines with `trip` null and write a `missing_receipt` finding naming the fare, the rides and their days and saying the ticket would show the trip, and ask which place they were for (step 4), never leaving them with the finding alone; a fare and the rides or meals of the days right after it date a stay, which that question carries to the city of each place it offers (step 4's Trips no document places paragraph) — unless the person's note names the city for those days, which then places the segment the rows date, or a trip the person declared covers those days, which ties them (below). One itinerary is one trip: a stop abroad (receipts there, such as a meal and a taxi) followed within a week by a stop in another city, with no leg or receipt back in the home city between them, is one trip with a segment per city, placed where either stop's documents point, and carried whole when a question's options carry it (step 4); otherwise each stop is its own trip. Segments of one trip never share a day (a leg that leaves one stop and lands at the next the same day starts the next segment, and the earlier one ends the day before), lie inside the tax year (a trip crossing its edge is recorded up to December 31 or from January 1, which its documents date only when one prints a day across the edge or the segment's other end is printed beside it), and number at most 12. Spell the location as `rules/ty<tax_year>/per_diem.json` does, "City, ST" or "City, Country". Write the days that date a segment into its document's `fields` as ISO days (`other.legs` with each leg's `date`, `other.arrival`, `other.departure`, `other.travel_dates`): the step-8 check dates a recorded trip by its documents' fields and the lines booked from them. A trip ties this batch's travel lines dated inside one of its segments — the stay, and the fares, rides and meals of those days in that city: set their `trip`. A line dated outside the trip's days, such as a fare bought weeks before, keeps its travel line with `trip` null (`check_outputs.js` refuses a tied line outside its trip's days), and the trip's days are never stretched to reach it: a fare's day is the day it was bought, not a day away. It books on the trip's place when a document of the trip lists it (the ticket whose legs date the trip), and is otherwise asked which place it was for (step 4). A trip the person declared — one `profile.json` stages, or one their confirmed `trip_register` answer adds — covers its days: it is the note that names the city, so tie to it every travel line of this batch on its place dated inside its segments (a meal, or a line on its place's travel line: the stay, the fares and the rides), a row that prints no city included, and record no other trip over those days; a line whose staged reading prints an address in none of its cities, or whose document another trip lists (a ticket bought then for that trip), keeps the rules above (`check_outputs.js` refuses an untied travel line of its place on a declared trip's days, `trip_declared_days_untied`). A trip you record carries `"travel": "flew"` when one of its `documents` is an `airline_receipt`, and no `travel` otherwise (`check_outputs.js` refuses a flight with no airline receipt among its documents, `trip_facts_invalid`).
- A trip you record may carry up to two `purpose_suggestions`, what its documents say the trip was for, each `{"text": …, "document": …}`: a few words naming the activity (`Client workshop at Meridian`, `Meeting the plumber at the house`, `Course: Advanced Bookkeeping`) and the one document of the trip whose reading names that activity or its counterparty — a conference or course registration, a contractor's invoice for the visit, a client's agenda, a course receipt. That is the only ground: the trip's city, its dates, the place it sits under, a hotel folio, a fare or a card row name no activity, so a trip whose documents are only those carries none — a folio printing the company that booked the stay may place the trip (step 5) but still names no activity, even one printing an event's room block. A suggestion whose document's reading names an event — the reader writes `other.event` on a conference, trade-show or course registration, badge or ticket, whatever type it reads the page as — names an event: mark it `"event": true`, its `text` that `other.event` exactly. `check_outputs.js` refuses an event suggestion whose document's reading names no event of its text. A trip whose suggestions all name that one event carries its name as its `purpose` and keeps them; any other suggestion never sets `purpose`, which stays `null` (step 5), and with two that differ the person picks one, or another purpose, on the Trips screen (`check_outputs.js` refuses any other purpose or suggestion, `trip_recorded_purpose` and `trip_purpose_suggestions`). Its text carries no figure, link or identifier, and only a trip you record carries suggestions — never a staged trip or one an answer adds.
- Vehicle expenses (fuel, service, insurance, a vehicle loan payment): carry `"vehicle": "<the vehicle's key in the profile>"`. Parking and tolls never carry `vehicle` — they stay on their own lines, in neither figure of the vehicle comparison. A vehicle-attributed line keeps its schedule line.
- A staged business's `aliases` (`profile.json`) are the other names it goes by on its documents: a document that prints one — a 1099's recipient, a statement's account holder, an invoice's letterhead — names that business exactly as if it printed the business's `name`, so book its lines there and ask nothing about the name, except a hinted reading that names another place than its hint, which the `hint_entity` bullet above governs.
- A staged rental's `listings` (`profile.json`) are the platforms and listing ids it is let through, as its payout statements print them (`Vrbo 4820193`, `Property 367001`; the platform word's case and a `#` or `:` before the id do not matter). A statement whose every printed listing is one staged rental's books to that rental with no question: a payout export, read by code or by you, whose listing or property id column prints only ids that rental carries books its lines there as if it printed the rental's `name`, except a hinted reading that names another place than its hint, which the `hint_entity` bullet above governs. A statement that also prints a listing no staged rental carries falls to the bullets above and step 4's found places. A staged `register` question in `open_questions.json` whose place `profile.json` now carries by the same name, alias, listing or address is not a found place any more: book this run's own lines for it to that place with no question, and step 4 re-asks the staged question, for the lines already confirmed and unassigned, as a placement to that place. A statement that prints the listings of two staged rentals books none of its lines to a place: leave `entity` null and step 4 asks which rental each belongs to (`check_outputs.js` refuses a line from it booked to a place no answer chose). The pass-through lines the payout-export bullet above asks about are still asked.
- A staged place's `aliases` and `listings` include those of the places the person folded into it, which `profile.json` no longer stages: a document printing one books there as the two bullets above say. A staged rental's `reserved_names` are the bare platform names of the places folded into it, which carry no listing id (`Airbnb`): such a name names none of the person's places and is never a new place's name. A statement that names its place by that platform name alone, printing no listing id, books none of its lines to a place by it, and step 4 asks which of the person's places they belong to as a `placement` question — never placed without asking, and never offered as a new place. A statement that prints a listing id is judged by its listings, as the bullet above says, whatever its platform: a listing no staged rental carries is a found rental, asked as step 4 asks any found place, and its register option names the listing (`Booking 367001`), never the reserved name alone (`check_outputs.js` refuses an `entity_register` option naming a reserved name).
- 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC and 1099-K income goes to the business it belongs to as `kind: income`. Other 1099s are `listed_only`.
- A K-1 books nothing: it is the partnership's or S corporation's finished form, and Bookette hands it to the accountant. Record it in `out/documents.json` as `schedule_k1` with status `support`, its `fields` carrying `vendor_or_payer` as printed, `tax_year` (the year the form is for) and `form_boxes` (each box keyed by the number and code the form prints, such as `"13 code W"`, never its label, with its printed amount alone, a loss signed: `"-1210"`); the export lists it for the accountant. Write no line, entity, question or finding for a K-1 or the partnership or S corporation it names; `check_outputs.js` refuses a line booked from a `schedule_k1` document and a change it arms.
- Donation receipts go to `out/donations.json` — one object per receipt, never to the ledger. Each entry carries: `organization`, `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`), `amount` (decimal string), `currency`, `form` (`cash` or `non_cash`), `goods_services_amount` (decimal string, or null when nothing was received in return), and `source_file` (the inbox file it came from). The build lists them for the accountant; they are not part of Schedule C or E books.
- A cost of the home the home-office reading is computed from goes to `out/home_costs.json` - one object per stated figure, never to the ledger, and a document that feeds the home costs feeds no ledger line: a utility bill, the home's insurance declaration, the home's mortgage interest statement (box 1 interest, box 10 taxes), a property-tax bill, rent receipts, repair invoices, or a card or bank export no ledger line cites. The shape and the field list are in `reference/profile.md`. Copy each stated figure with its `date`, `amount`, `currency` and `source_file`; give it a `kind` (`mortgage_interest`, `real_estate_taxes`, `insurance`, `rent`, `utilities`, `repairs` or `other`) and a `use` (`home` for a cost of the whole home, `office` for a cost of the office alone). The document a home cost is copied from is `booked` in `out/documents.json`, as a bill a staged payment names is: `check_outputs.js` counts a home cost as booking its `source_file`, though the build books neither home-office reading on a ledger line (step 6). Never sum, never split a loan payment and never record principal. A bill that combines kinds (gas and electric) is copied once, at its stated total, under one kind. Stage the home's costs whenever a home office exists or this batch proposes one — an entity's `home_office`, or a floor plan, a lease, a listing or an appraisal this batch reads — even before the place is registered: a document shaped as a cost of a home is never `personal` on the ground that no home office is staged yet, and when a later run's answer or registration gives an entity its `home_office`, the home-cost documents that run can still read — its batch's, or a reading still staged under `extractions/` — are staged from. The regular reading needs the home's area and these costs: an input no document gives is asked for (the `home_office` fact, step 4) or named by the `missing_receipt` finding the build's row prints, never silently missing. A home cost inside a card or bank export that also carries business transactions is not staged: write a `missing_receipt` finding naming it, so the person can upload the bill itself.
- A property-tax payment goes to `out/tax_payments.json` — one object per payment, never to the ledger; the shape and the full field list are in `reference/profile.md`. Carry `payment_source` (the inbox file the payment evidence came from), `date` (the payment date — the payment is booked from it), `amount`, `currency`, `bill_source` (the inbox file of the bill), `parcels` (the bill's parcels with their stated amounts), and `covered_parcels` (empty when the payment covers the whole bill). Copy the bill's stated figures; never divide — the build splits the payment across the parcels, the stated amounts as weights, and books each piece into the receiving entity's taxes line. The bill's property location (its situs) identifies the property; an owner or mailing address never does. The bill of the home the home office sits in is a home cost (`real_estate_taxes`), never a tax payment, and its parcel goes on no entity.
